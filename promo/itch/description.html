Classic Sudoku — plus variant rules you can mix and match.

A clean, offline number puzzle. Play plain Sudoku, or switch on up to two variant rules and combine them for a fresh kind of grid. Every puzzle has a single solution and can be cracked by logic alone — no guessing — with difficulty graded by a real solver, not a random clue count. There is exactly one deliberate exception, and it is clearly marked: see Blank mode below.

Variants (pick up to two, roll them with Random, or play Classic)

  • Diagonal — both main diagonals must also hold 1–9
  • Hyper — four extra 3×3 boxes must also hold 1–9
  • Killer — dashed cages add up to the small number in their corner, with no digit repeated inside
  • Antiknight — cells a knight's move apart can't share a digit
  • Antiking — cells touching diagonally can't share a digit
  • Nonconsecutive — cells sharing an edge can't hold consecutive digits
  • Disjoint Groups — the same position within each box holds nine different digits
  • Jigsaw — nine irregular regions replace the 3×3 boxes
  • Clone — two same-shaped regions hold the same digits, cell for cell
  • Even/Odd — shaded squares must hold an even digit, circles an odd one
  • Kropki — a white dot joins consecutive neighbours, a black dot two where one is double the other
  • XV — X joins two cells that add to 10, V two that add to 5
  • Thermo — digits rise along each thermometer, from the bulb to the tip
  • Palindrome — each marked line reads the same in both directions
  • German Whispers — neighbours along the line differ by at least 5
  • Renban — each marked line holds a run of consecutive digits, in any order
  • Zipper — on a zipper line, pairs an equal step from the centre add up to the centre
  • Arrow — the circled cell holds the sum of the digits along its tail
  • Sandwich — the number outside a row or column is the sum of everything between the 1 and the 9
  • Little Killer — an arrow outside the grid gives the sum along that whole diagonal, where digits may repeat

Blank mode

Pick Little Killer on its own at Hard and the grid arrives with no given digits at all — every scrap of information sits in the margins. This is the one board in the game that can't be finished by pure logic: the four corner clues each pin a single cell, and past that it takes trial and backtracking. It's here for anyone who wants the most arithmetic a grid can carry.

How to play

  • Fill — tap a cell, then tap a number (or type 1–9)
  • Notes — pencil-mark candidates before you commit
  • Color — shade cells in any of nine colors to track your logic
  • Hint — reveals the next logical step: first the technique and region, then the exact cell
  • Undo & Erase — step back or clear a cell anytime
  • Auto-save — close it and pick up right where you left off
